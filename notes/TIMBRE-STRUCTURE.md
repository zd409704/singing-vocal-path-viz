# What determines timbre and vocal identity, and how complete the current synthesizer is

"The engine" is `third_party/chimera-upgrade/` with `tools/chimera_voice_upgrade.patch` (sha256 `da7146ee…`), the planners `tools/make_upgrade_notes.py` and `tools/make_nursery_notes.py`, the room in `tools/finish_upgrade_audio.py`, and the nine rules in `notes/SYNTH-ALGO-ROOTFIX.md`. ## Short answer

1. **Timbre** at an instant is set by the glottal pulse, the noise at the glottis and constrictions, the tract's shape and losses, and radiation. **Vocal identity** is what stays put across notes and words: anatomy, the source's habits as a function of pitch and loudness, motor habits, and the long-term spectrum those produce.
2. **The synthesizer is not complete.** Of the 47 structures listed in section 2, 5 are modeled, 13 approximated, 16 held as a preset or constant, and 13 absent. Its strength is the aeroacoustic middle of the ladder: a real branched airway, closures, flow-driven noise. Its weak ends are the folds, which are a prescribed area curve with no tissue, register or self-oscillation, and the person, who is a few constants with no growth, no breath supply and no measured identity.
3. **From small to large**, each scale adds something the one below cannot produce. The engine has most of the steps from pulse through tract to phrase shape, though several are supplied by formula from outside. It lacks the steps where one scale acts back on another: tract on fold vibration, pitch on tract shape, air supply on phrase, and anatomy on pitch range.

## 1. Which structures do the work

### Timbre: why this instant has this color

| Structure | What it does to the sound |
| --- | --- |
| Fold closure (open quotient, closing speed, closure completeness) | Sets source spectral tilt: how much energy reaches upper harmonics, and H1−H2 |
| Glottal leakage and aspiration | Adds a noise floor locked to the cycle, and damps the tract |
| Cycle-to-cycle irregularity | Sets harmonic-to-noise ratio and roughness |
| Tract area function (tongue, jaw, lips, larynx height, pharynx) | Places the formants, so sets vowel color |
| Velum and nasal cavity | Adds resonances and anti-resonances |
| Laryngeal and hypopharyngeal cavities | Shape the region above about the third formant |
| Yielding walls and viscous loss | Set formant bandwidth and low-frequency behaviour |
| Constriction and teeth turbulence | Produce frication spectra |
| Lips and radiation | Differentiate flow, adding high-frequency emphasis that depends on opening |
| Room and microphone | Outside the body, but in every recording |

### Voice quality and identity: why it is the same person

| Family | Structures | Why it persists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anatomy | Tract length and proportions, cavity shapes above F3, nasal geometry, fold length and mass | Bone and tissue change slowly |
| Laryngeal habits | Typical closure, leakage and tilt, and how these change with pitch and loudness | Learned and physiological; the mapping is the habit, not any one value |
| Motor habits | Articulatory setting (habitual jaw, tongue and larynx posture), consonant timing, coarticulation, attack, vibrato | Practice and language background |
| Breath habits | Where and how often the singer breathes, pressure shape over a phrase | Lung capacity and training |
| Long-term summaries | Long-term average spectrum, pitch distribution | Consequences of all of the above, not causes |

Timbre and identity use many of the same structures. The difference is the time scale of the question. Tension on one note is timbre. The function from pitch to tension across a career is identity. A synthesizer can therefore get timbre right frame by frame and still have no identity, if each frame's setting is not generated by a stable personal mapping.

## 2. Completeness inventory

Status terms:

- **Modeled**: computed from physics or geometry, and varies with its causes.
- **Approximated**: present and varying, but by formula or outside plan rather than from its cause.
- **Preset**: a fixed value or a value per body. Having a number is not having the structure.
- **Lacking**: no representation.

Status describes the path that rendered the songs: `render notes` at 48 kHz with 2× oversampling, the `bodyMix` body, `phonologyRules` on (`crates/chimera-cli/src/render.rs:534-542`).

### Overall judgment

**Not complete.** "Complete" here means every listed structure varies as it does in a person, and that holds for only 5 of 47. Those are the airway, closures and gesture overlap, which is the part that made this engine worth choosing over the formant synth. A further 13 vary but through formula or outside plan. 16 are numbers that do not move, and 13 have no representation.

The most consequential gaps are, in order of my judgment rather than measurement:

1. No fold tissue, and no tract-to-fold feedback, so no register.
2. No stable absolute-pitch source mapping.
3. No pitch-dependent tract adjustment.
4. No air supply.
5. No individual articulatory setting or checked identity.

The older formant synth is further away on every row with an airway or closure in it, and no better on the fold rows.

Still open from earlier notes: the wasm build and app were not rebuilt, the rules are off for speech and live notes, and absolute H1−H2 remains about 8 dB below upstream's singer on Amazing Grace (`notes/COMPARISON-voice-upgrade.md`).

## 3. From small scale to large scale

### 3.1 Physical ladder

**Tissue and one glottal cycle.** Layered, viscoelastic folds collide and peel apart. The new behaviour is a pulse with a return phase and incomplete closure, and small cycle variation arising from the tissue. Below this level there is no sound. *Engine:* the outcome is drawn, not produced. The pulse is a cosine shape with parameters, and irregularity is added noise. **Step absent; result imitated.**

**Folds, pressure and the source pulse.** Pressure and tension together set whether the folds vibrate, how fast, and with what closure. The new behaviour is a coupled map: more pressure raises level, sharpens closure and shifts pitch. *Engine:* pressure drives effort, effort shapes the pulse, and tension is scheduled. Pressure does not move pitch, and pitch does not move threshold. The pitch-to-tension link exists only in the planner and is relative to each song: `base + 0.025 × (midi − song mean)`.

That has a measurable consequence. G4 receives tension 0.473 in Amazing Grace, 0.522 in Twinkle and 0.483 in Two Tigers, derived from the score means 66.50, 64.52 and 66.06. The difference is small, about two semitones' worth, but a real habit would give one value. **Step partly present, outside the engine.**

**Articulators, tract, formants, nasality, radiation.** Movable structures turn one source into many vowels and consonants. New behaviours are formants, anti-resonances, closures with pressure build-up and release, and coarticulation. A source alone can do none of these. *Engine:* this is its strongest rung. Airway, closures, velum port and flow noise are computed. **Step present**, limited to 1-D acoustics and population-mean targets.

**Register, source-tract coupling, pitch-dependent adjustment.** The folds and tract now act as one system. New behaviours are register breaks and mixes, instabilities when harmonics cross formants, and singers moving the tract to follow pitch. At these pitches this is where the separate source and filter accounts stop being enough. *Engine:* flow feels the tract, which is the only coupling. The folds do not, no register exists, and no tract target depends on f0. **Step mostly absent.**

The evidence is suggestive. Using the same source settings, H1−H2 from notes at or below D4 to notes at or above G4 goes −4.3 to −6.3 dB on Amazing Grace, but −4.4 to +2.7 on Twinkle and −4.4 to −3.0 on Two Tigers (`results/voice_upgrade/comparison.json`, `results/nursery/comparison.json`, renders before the algorithm root fix). So the 2.0 dB fall that Stage C reached does not carry to other songs. Twinkle rises by 7.1 dB. Which harmonics meet which vowel's F1 is a plausible cause, and is a **guess**: note sets and vowels differ, and it was not tested.

**Breath, phrase and performance.** The new behaviour is organisation in time: finite air shapes phrase length and pressure, and intent shapes arcs and endings. *Engine:* the shape is applied by formula and inhales go through the airway, but air is unlimited. A phrase cannot run out, tire the voice or change quality because of what came before. Each phrase's formula knows only its own notes. **Step present as a shape; the cause is absent.**

**Body scale and identity.** The new behaviour is invariance: one set of structures generating every note. It is a property of the whole mapping over a long time and is not visible in any single frame. *Engine:* one anatomy is held through a song and shared across three songs, so the constants are consistent. Consistent constants are necessary but are not an identity. Nothing here shows the voice would be recognized as the same across songs, because no such test has been run. **Step present only as constants.**

### 3.2 Engine control ladder

| Rung | Where | What it provides | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sample | `human_voice.rs:render` | Airway, area, flow, noise | Fold mechanics |
| Control block (64 × oversample samples) | `control` | Articulators, slew, contacts, effort, pitch glide | Per-cycle tissue state; f0 feedback |
| Phone gesture | `planner.rs`, inventory | Targets, overlap, nine rules | Pitch-dependent and speaker-specific targets |
| Note | `sing.rs`, planner `tension`, `vibrato`, `approaches` | Timing, per-note tension and vibrato | Absolute-pitch mapping, downward shapes, tied vibrato and pressure |
| Phrase | planner `pressure`, `inhales` | Arc, final decay, inhale | Air budget; phrase-to-phrase state |
| Song | `SOURCES`, `BODY_MIX`, seed, room | One body and one source | Larger-scale form, such as a verse sung differently; fatigue |

The upper four rungs live mostly in Python and only reach the engine as gestures, so nothing the sound does can influence them. Control flows down the ladder only. That is the common form of most gaps above.

## 4. Developmental scale: child to adult

Growth changes the same structures, not independently. Qualitatively, from established physiology:

- Children have short folds whose layered structure is still developing, a small and high larynx, a relatively short pharynx, small lungs, and high pitch.
- With growth the tract lengthens and formants fall. The larynx descends and the pharynx lengthens more than the oral cavity, so the proportions change as well as the size.
- At puberty, male folds lengthen far more than female folds, which is why male pitch drops more than tract growth alone would predict.
- Motor control also matures: children's timing is more variable, and breath is shorter.

Comparison:

- The presets do capture one real fact: tract proportions change with body, because pharynx scales apart from k. Vowel targets are calibrated separately per preset, using data that scale by 1.17 to 1.20 for women and 1.24 to 1.33 for children by default (`vowel_data.rs:26-27`), so articulation compensates for what k alone would give.
- They do not capture growth. The body is a point on a line between presets. A child is one fixed point rather than a trajectory. All geometry moves together with weights, so there is no separate timing for laryngeal descent or for pharynx growth.
- Fold size follows k through one rule. In humans fold-length differences between sexes are larger than tract differences, so one k cannot represent both. The folds' size does not set pitch either: the child body would sing any MIDI note it is given.
- Lung pressure (9.0 in `render.rs:539`), the phonation threshold, wall constants, jitter and articulator speeds do not depend on body. The output gain ramp (0.55 to 1) is a level correction, not growth, and is 0.6175 at the mix.

**The Stage C mix** (k 0.915, pharynx 0.898) is a point 15 % of the way from female toward male along that line. It is an adult fictional tract, 1.7 % longer than the female preset, and it is not on any child-to-adult path. It is not a model of a particular age or person. On the nursery songs (MIDI 58 to 72, about 233 to 523 Hz) it is singing a child's repertoire with an adult tract, as chosen by the nursery decision. The child preset has not been rendered for these songs, and whether it would suit them is unmeasured.

Vowel targets for the mix are interpolated articulator targets, not a recalibration. `crates/chimera/tests/body_mix.rs` checks that they lie between the presets; it does not check resulting formants. No formant data for the mix are recorded in this program.

## 6. Honest limits

- Nobody listened as part of this analysis, and no voice quality claim is made.
- No human recording was measured. Upstream's singer figures are from other songs and another tool.
- Status labels are judgment calls against the definitions in section 2. Reasonable readers might move individual rows between Approximated and Modeled. The conclusion does not depend on any single row.
- Cross-song H1−H2 comparisons use the earlier `_upgrade` nursery renders, measured with different note sets and vowels. They were not re-measured on `_algo` renders.
- Identity has not been measured at all: no speaker-similarity, long-term-spectrum stability or listener test was run. Consistency here means only that the constants are shared.
- 2-D-to-1-D limits were inferred from the code, and no comparison against a 3-D simulation was done.
- Physiology is qualitative, and the developmental section's anatomy is general knowledge; none of it was measured here.
- No code was changed, and nothing was rendered, committed or pushed.
